### mt/q006 | skills=mcq_cs
@user
What is the time complexity of binary search on a sorted list of n items?
A. O(1)
B. O(log n)
C. O(n)
D. O(n log n)
@assistant
Binary search looks at the middle item and discards half of the remaining list at each step. Starting from n items, the list shrinks to n/2, n/4, n/8 and so on, until one item is left. The number of halvings needed is about log2 of n.

O(n) would be a linear scan that checks every item, O(1) would mean a fixed number of steps for any size, and O(n log n) is typical of good sorting algorithms.

Answer: B
@check has=Answer: B
@user
How many comparisons at most for a list of 1,000,000 items?
A. About 20
B. About 1,000
C. About 500,000
D. 1,000,000
@assistant
Step 1: The number of steps is about log2 of 1,000,000.
Step 2: 2 to the power 10 is 1,024, roughly a thousand, so 2 to the power 20 is roughly a million.
Step 3: That gives about 20 comparisons.

Option C is the average for a linear scan, and D is its worst case.

Answer: A
@check has=Answer: A
@user
What if the list isn't sorted?
@assistant
Then binary search does not work. Its logic depends on knowing that everything to the left of the middle is smaller and everything to the right is larger. On an unsorted list, discarding half could throw away the item you are looking for.

You have two options:
- Search once: use a linear scan, which is O(n).
- Search many times: sort the list first, which is O(n log n), and then each search is O(log n). A hash set is another choice and gives roughly constant-time lookups.
@check has=linear
