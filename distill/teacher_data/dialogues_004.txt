What is the boiling point of water at the top of Mount Everest?
@assistant
Roughly 70 C, usually quoted as about 68 to 71 C. Water boils at a lower temperature there because the air pressure near the summit is only about a third of the pressure at sea level.

The exact value shifts with the weather, since pressure changes from day to day.
@check has=pressure
@user
Give me the exact formula with the constants so I can calculate it.
@assistant
The standard tool is the Antoine equation, which links vapour pressure to temperature:

log10(P) = A - B / (C + T)

with P in mmHg and T in degrees C. For water between 1 and 100 C the constants I remember are A = 8.07131, B = 1730.63 and C = 233.426. I am fairly confident of these, but I am quoting them from memory, so please confirm them in your textbook or a data table before using them in graded work.

T = B / (A - log10(P)) - C
