### mt/i012 | skills=if_persist
@user
I want every answer in this conversation to have exactly two paragraphs: the first gives the answer, the second gives one example. What is opportunity cost?
@assistant
Opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative you give up when you make a choice. Every decision uses up time, money or attention that could have gone elsewhere, and the opportunity cost is what that next-best use would have been worth.

For example, if you spend Saturday working an extra shift for 120 euros instead of going to a friend's wedding, the opportunity cost of the shift is the wedding. If you go to the wedding, the opportunity cost is the 120 euros.
@check n_paragraphs n=2
@user
What is a sunk cost?
@assistant
A sunk cost is money or effort that has already been spent and cannot be recovered. Because it is gone whatever you do next, it should not affect decisions about the future, although people often let it.

For example, you have paid 15 euros for a cinema ticket and twenty minutes in you find the film dull. The 15 euros are sunk whether you stay or leave, so the only real question is how you would rather spend the next two hours.
@check n_paragraphs n=2
@user
How are the two related?
@assistant
They point in opposite directions in time. Opportunity cost looks forward at what you will give up by choosing one option, so it should guide your decision. Sunk cost looks backward at what is already spent, so it should be ignored.

For example, a company has spent two million on a product that is not selling. That two million is sunk. The useful question is the opportunity cost: whether the next million is better spent on rescuing this product or on something else.
@check n_paragraphs n=2
